What I do

Not a channel. The whole commercial system.

Companies rarely call me about a channel. They call because something at the business level stopped working: growth flattened while spend kept rising, a category is being lost in search and nobody can say when it started, a seven-figure ad budget has no owner who can defend it, a launch into a new market has to land the first time, or a name that carries the company is being defined by whatever a search engine decides to show.

Those look like five different problems. They are usually one. The commercial side of the business was assembled channel by channel, each with its own vendor, its own definition of a conversion and its own report, so no single view exists of how a euro becomes revenue. Fix a channel inside that and you get a better-looking dashboard, not a better business.

So I work at the system level. Every engagement opens with an end-to-end audit — the business analytics and the measurement first — because a plan built on unverified data is a guess with a deck around it. Then the work gets sequenced in the order that returns money soonest, with a named owner on every part and a number everyone in the room has agreed to. I plug in where the system is missing, which is not always at the top.

Business consulting

Commercial system design: which parts of the business are actually load-bearing, where margin is leaking, what to sequence first, and what to stop paying for. The audit comes before the roadmap, every time — the same anamnesis a doctor takes before prescribing anything.

Enterprise SEO

Organic search treated as infrastructure, not a content calendar. Crawl and index control, rendering, site architecture, internal link equity, schema, log-file analysis and hreflang across multi-market deployments — plus visibility inside the AI answer engines that now intercept the query before a SERP ever loads. I ran analytics and SEO for the World Economic Forum in Davos, and enterprise search systems in 10+ markets.

Paid media oversight

Governance over large ad spends. Not another hand on the bidding — the accountability layer above it: conversion tracking verified before a campaign launches, Ads-to-GA4 match rates monitored monthly with anything under 90% triggering an attribution audit, dead conversion actions stripped out of Smart Bidding, budget pacing reviewed weekly and overspend flagged inside 24 hours. Smart Bidding is only ever as good as the signal underneath it, and on first-pass audits that signal is usually broken.

Reputation management

Campaigns for high-profile business figures and the companies attached to their names. What surfaces when someone searches a name before a meeting, an investment or a hire — owned assets, entity and knowledge-graph control, and the results that outrank whatever showed up on its own. Measured like any other channel, discussed publicly far less than any other channel.

Go-to-market

For industrial and tech firms in particular: ICP built on verified firmographic and behavioural data, positioning, messaging architecture that sales and marketing both work from, channel selection with a stated rationale, and launch sequencing with owners and checkpoints. Launches rarely fail on product. They fail because two teams were aiming at different companies and nobody noticed until CAC said so.

Analytics and attribution

The layer everything else stands on. GA4 implementation and event taxonomy, tracking audits, multi-touch attribution, and reporting architecture that maps to the P&L rather than to pageviews. One definition per metric, one source of truth, every euro tied to a measurable result — then a monthly diagnostic loop so it stays honest as the work compounds.

AI-native operations

Semi-autonomous marketing systems: agentic workflows running reporting, monitoring and QA in the background, so a small senior team covers ground that used to need twenty people. Every business will soon run more AI than it can staff. The ones that grow will have the clearest owners, not the most agents.

The work runs through Funky Enterprises, the commercial system partner I co-founded — five senior operators, no employees, no juniors learning on your account. We own the strategy and the build, and we would rather share in what the system produces than bill you by the hour.

Why the center.

Every partner in the firm got a position on the floor. I am the center, number five. I hold the paint: the measurement, the attribution, the governance under everything else. Nobody applauds the center. A rebound never makes the highlight reel, a blocked shot rarely decides a story about the game. But no offense runs without someone holding the back of it down, and no play works if the paint is open.

It is the same instinct in the work. I would rather own the unglamorous layer everyone assumes is already handled, and be the person in the room who can say what a number means and then defend it when the answer is inconvenient. Belgrade is home, and CET is the timezone I actually keep, most hours of it.
