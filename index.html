What I do

Nobody owns the numbers.

Most companies I meet do not have a marketing problem. They have a governance problem. Four dashboards, three definitions of a conversion, and a last-click report quietly deciding next quarter's budget. Everyone is measuring. Nobody agrees.

Automation makes it worse before it makes it better. An AI stack laid over ungoverned operations does not produce clarity, it produces more output that nobody can trace back to revenue. Marketing operations have to be governed before they can be automated or scaled. That is the principle I built the firm on.

So I work underneath the strategy: the measurement, the attribution, the governance that everything else stands on. I define the numbers once, rebuild the reporting so it maps to the P&L, and hand the team a system that tells them what to do next instead of what already happened. Every euro gets an owner and a result.

Measurement architecture

Analytics, tracking and data governance built once, documented, and owned by a name. One definition per metric, one source of truth, and no quarterly argument about whose number is right.

Attribution

Every euro tied to a measurable result. Models that survive contact with a board meeting: what a channel actually contributed, what it merely touched, and what you would lose if you switched it off tomorrow.

Enterprise SEO governance

Organic as infrastructure, not a content calendar. Crawl, index, architecture and internal linking at marketplace and catalog scale, with the technical debt paid down in the order that returns money first.

Owned media

The assets you do not rent. Site, content, search and the audience that arrives without a bid, priced honestly against the paid channels it is supposed to replace.

AI and operations

Agentic workflows that run reporting, monitoring and QA in the background. Every business will soon run more AI than it can staff. The ones that grow will have the clearest owners, not the most agents.

The work runs through Funky Enterprises, a five-partner commercial system practice. Strategy, creative, performance, commerce and owned media, with every name on the work and no juniors learning on your account.

Off the clock

Why the center.

Every partner in the firm got a position on the floor. I am the center, number five. I hold the paint: the measurement, the attribution, the governance under everything else. Nobody applauds the center. A rebound never makes the highlight reel, a blocked shot rarely decides a story about the game. But no offense runs without someone holding the back of it down, and no play works if the paint is open.

It is the same instinct in the work. I would rather own the unglamorous layer everyone assumes is already handled, and be the person in the room who can say what a number means and then defend it when the answer is inconvenient. Belgrade is home, and CET is the timezone I actually keep, most hours of it.
